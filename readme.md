# Official code and data for{Learning the Boundary of the Solvable: Alignment of LLMs with Problem Unsolvability}

Ensuring the reliability of Large Language Models (LLMs) requires not only the ability to solve complex problems but also the discernment to recognize when a problem cannot be solved. Current models often fail to distinguish between \textit{objective unsolvability} (inherent logical contradictions) and \textit{subjective capability limitations} (difficulty beyond current reach), leading to hallucinations on impossible tasks or overconfidence on hard ones. To address this, we propose a comprehensive alignment framework that treats these as orthogonal dimensions, establishing a robust \textbf{three-way decision boundary}: (1) accurately solving feasible problems; (2) explicitly detecting inherent contradictions; and (3) prudently abstaining from tasks exceeding current capabilities via self-calibration. To bridge the data gap, we construct \textbf{UnsolvableQA}, a dataset derived via a novel ``Reverse Construction'' methodology that generates unsolvable instances by injecting controlled contradictions into the Chain-of-Thought of solvable problems. By leveraging Reinforcement Learning with a \textbf{dynamic confidence threshold}, we enable models to adaptively balance objective detection and subjective calibration. Empirical results demonstrate a \textbf{synergistic improvement}: our approach achieves near-perfect unsolvability detection while simultaneously boosting reasoning accuracy on solvable tasks. Crucially, we identify the phenomenon of \textbf{Capability Collapse}, revealing that explicit exposure to inherently unsolvable data is indispensable for preventing models from degenerating into overconfident guessers.
